All images opened, every image had a readable label file, and no class ID or substantial box-coordinate error was found. The source rounds normalized YOLO coordinates to six decimal places. **94 boxes exceed an image boundary by at most 0.000005** when reconstructed from those rounded values. The validator allows 0.00001 for this rounding effect without changing source labels; larger errors remain rejected.

Class counts are uneven: the supplied training split has 2,003 `boots` boxes but only 265 `no_goggle` boxes and 275 `forklift` boxes. That makes per-class recall and support more informative than overall mAP alone.

The audit found **eight average-hash cross-split candidate pairs** involving two training images and four validation images. I reviewed the six distinct images in `artifacts/near_duplicate_review.jpg`. They depict different worksite scenes; similar broad skies and backgrounds caused the hash matches. There were no exact cross-split duplicates. Average hash is only a candidate finder, so a broader visual or embedding-based duplicate review remains useful before the final report.

## Ten-image visual review

The reproducible contact sheet is `artifacts/annotated_samples.jpg`, generated by `python -m ppe_detector samples data/construction-ppe`. The ten sampled image names are emitted by that command. Visible observations:

1. Several scenes contain many small PPE boxes on distant people. The source images are often 2048 pixels on one side; reducing them to 416 pixels for a 4 GB GPU may make helmets, gloves, and goggles hard to detect.
2. Missing-equipment labels mark visible body regions; they can sit close to positive equipment boxes for another person. Error inspection must consider which person and body region each box belongs to.
3. Some generated-looking and collected-looking scenes differ strongly in composition. The dataset card confirms a mix of generated and human-collected images, so a score on this archive alone cannot establish real-site reliability.

These are observations from the ten sampled images, not a measured model result or a complete label-quality assessment.

## Frozen evaluation policy

`python -m ppe_detector split data/construction-ppe --output artifacts/split` produced **680 train, 120 development, 200 final test** with seed `20261104`. The 200 published validation images are reserved for the final test; all model selection uses the 120-image development set. The source archive is unmodified. The split manifest and image lists live under ignored `artifacts/split/` and can be regenerated from the recorded seed and ZIP hash.

All 12 classes are represented in the development set; its smallest class is `forklift` with 41 boxes. The frozen split contains 10,322 training and 1,580 development boxes.

The development and final-test sets come from the same mixed-source archive. The final report must say that this is a held-out dataset test, not an independent deployment-environment test.
